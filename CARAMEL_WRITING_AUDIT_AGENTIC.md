# Caramel writing audit for the agentic studio

**Status:** shared Caramel writing reference
**Scope:** website copy, SEO articles, social posts, proposals, invoices, forms, client replies, agent handovers, chatbot copy, translations, and future application interfaces.

## 1. Voice and ownership

Caramel Digital Studio is a solo developer studio supported by agents. Write from that structure.

- Use **I** for Melissa’s work, judgement, availability, delivery, and owner decisions.
- Use **Caramel Digital Studio** for the studio, service catalogue, website, systems, and published work.
- Use **agent**, **developer**, **orchestrator**, **client**, **owner**, or **person** for a specific role.
- Use **we** only when a named collaboration genuinely includes more than one person or when quoting approved source text.
- Describe agents as tools and delivery support. Keep judgement, authorship, ethics, approval, and responsibility with the owner.
- Describe the studio as a focused solo practice with agent-supported capability. Avoid dinosaur-agency language, team claims, departments, account managers, or invented capacity.

## 2. Core writing standard

Write fresh, precise, elegant English with a clear subject and a strong verb. Give each paragraph one job. Choose concrete nouns over abstract business language. Prefer a sentence that names the page, service, person, file, price, action, or result.

Use UK English for UK and South African audiences. Use US English for US audiences. Record the audience and language variant before drafting. Keep spelling, punctuation, terminology, dates, currency, and capitalisation consistent.

Use commas, full stops, colons, and semicolons. Use hyphens only where grammar requires them. Use an em dash zero times in public copy, handovers, client messages, and agent outputs. Review copied quotations separately and mark them as quotations.

## 3. Positive directness

Describe the present service, system, evidence, constraint, price, decision, or next action. State a material constraint once as a clear rule, then return to the useful path.

Keep competitor framing, fear framing, exclusion-led copy, artificial urgency, inflated claims, and comparison slogans out of new writing. Replace generic contrast with a direct description of the approved offer.

Restricted wording includes `not`, `real`, `actual`, and `human`, except where a precise quotation, technical term, role reference, review reference, or ethical discussion requires the word. Rewrite the sentence first. Record a justified exception during review.

## 4. Freshness and substance

Before delivery, remove repeated ideas, padded introductions, generic conclusions, filler transitions, stock AI phrases, empty adjectives, and claims without evidence. Vary sentence length and paragraph rhythm. Keep headings useful. Give every list a clear purpose.

Use named proof: a source file, verified number, approved price, observed behaviour, client decision, test result, or owner-supplied fact. Mark assumptions, placeholders, dependencies, and open decisions clearly.

## 5. Five-pass audit

1. **Purpose:** identify the audience, action, page, format, and owner decision.
2. **Meaning:** confirm every claim, price, process, provider, and example against an approved source.
3. **Voice:** check solo-studio ownership, pronouns, role language, calm confidence, and Caramel specificity.
4. **Language:** check grammar, spelling, punctuation, terminology, banned patterns, repeated phrases, long em dashes, and unnecessary negative wording.
5. **Delivery:** check headings, links, calls to action, translation readiness, accessibility, and the next action for the reader or agent.

A writing pass ends with a clean copy sample and a short audit note listing the source, audience, language, checks, and open decision.

## 6. Translation and African-language localisation

Translate meaning, intent, tone, terminology, and action. Translate from an approved source copy rather than from a rough draft. Keep names, prices, URLs, placeholders, provider names, legal wording, and technical identifiers stable.

Use a qualified language reviewer or an African-language model for Afrikaans, isiXhosa, isiZulu, and any later selected language. Record the chosen language, regional variant, reviewer, source version, and approval state. Preserve natural grammar and cultural clarity. Avoid word-for-word transfer when it damages meaning.

Select languages through audience evidence, reach, service area, and review capacity. Keep the language set focused enough for accurate maintenance. The African-language LLM can support translation research and community contribution, while owner review remains the publication gate.

## 7. Caramel, Gaia, and future decision layers

Apply this audit to Caramel work. Apply Gaia-specific mission, thesis, ethics, learning, and research language through the Lotus OS Gaia skill. Share clarity, evidence, security, and owner review across both domains. Keep domain-specific identity and audience separate.

Future JEV, CLEF, or other decision layers belong in a later application architecture. Define each model’s purpose, inputs, outputs, confidence, evidence, reviewer, escalation path, and audit record before implementation. Keep decision layers separate from the current static site and from copy approval.

## 8. Final release gate

Release writing after the owner-approved source is identified, the five passes are complete, the audience and language are recorded, all links and claims are checked, and the handover states the exact next action. A long em dash, unexplained team voice, invented claim, repeated filler, or unreviewed translation sends the copy back to editing.
